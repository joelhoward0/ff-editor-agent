# Voice card prompt

Read three chapters the author wrote themselves. Write a VOICE CARD: how this
author ACTUALLY writes (not how a style guide says they should), precise
enough that another writer could imitate them. 600–900 words.

Cover, counting rather than guessing wherever you can:

- **Distance and attention:** POV, tense, what the narration notices (logistics?
  bodies? weather?), how thought is rendered.
- **Register:** slang, profanity, internet-speak, endearments, jokes; does the
  narration slip into the characters' speech register?
- **Punctuation:** dash style (em dash, spaced or not, or spaced hyphen),
  straight vs curly quotes, ellipsis character, italics, exclamation rate.
  Include looseness that's part of the texture, but don't instruct anyone to
  copy outright typos.
- **Rhythm:** sentence mean/median, share of very short sentences, what long
  sentences are built from ("and"-chains vs subordinate clauses), paragraph
  length, one-line beats, section-break style.
- **Dialogue:** tags (says/said/none), pacing, how side characters talk.
- **Special devices** of this story (telepathy, texts, flashbacks): exactly how
  they're typeset and tagged.
- **Action and emotion:** blunt or choreographed? Is emotion named outright or
  shown? Sentimental or restrained?
- **Openings, endings, humor.**
- **10–15 short quotes** (under 15 words each) that show the voice.
- **What imitators get wrong:** usually too polished, too literary, too
  restrained, too cool. Be specific to this author.
